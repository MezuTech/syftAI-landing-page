features:
  - title: A reel, not a wall of text
    body: >-
      One verse fills the screen, in a serif set for reading rather than
      skimming. Swipe for the next. The gesture is the one your thumb already
      knows; what it moves through is the thing that rewards slowing down.
  - title: Tap any word for its root
    body: >-
      Every word is a link into the concordance — the Greek or Hebrew behind
      the wording, what it meant where it was written, and every other verse
      that uses the same root, with the English each translator reached for.
  - title: Four ways into a passage
    body: >-
      Plain Words puts the verse in everyday language. Word Roots goes to the
      original. Related Scriptures finds the passages carrying the same
      thought. Full Study is all three plus context, in order. Where a lens has
      nothing honest to add, it shows the gap rather than filling it.
  - title: The chapter, when the verse is not enough
    body: >-
      A reel is for arriving somewhere. The reader is for staying: the full
      chapter, continuous, with the verse you came from still marked and your
      place kept when you leave.
  - title: Plans sized to the time you have
    body: >-
      Pick a plan from four minutes to a full chapter. Miss a day and the plan
      bends rather than breaks — today is the next thing you have not read, not
      whatever the calendar says you should have.
  - title: Or build your own
    body: >-
      Choose the books and the pace and the app schedules the days for you. A
      plan you made is the same object as one that shipped with the app, and
      behaves the same everywhere.
  - title: Search that survives half-remembered wording
    body: >-
      Exact phrasing first, then partial. When what you remember is a
      paraphrase rather than anything the text actually says, assisted search
      finds the reference — and the verse then comes from our own database, so
      what you read is never something a model wrote.
  - title: Somewhere to start when you have no reference
    body: >-
      Discover is a deck of verse studies you move through a card at a time,
      filtered by the themes people actually arrive with — Peace & Anxiety,
      Grief & Comfort, Faith & Doubt, Provision & Work. Between the cards sit
      short reflections from writers whose work is out of copyright, each one
      credited to the book it came from.
  - title: A private journal, per verse
    body: >-
      Notes are free, need no account, and live on your device. Nobody reads
      them, including us. Pro carries them to your account so a reinstall or a
      second phone does not lose them.
  - title: Everything you kept, in one place
    body: >-
      Saved verses and highlights collect into a library you can come back to,
      dated the way a journal is — recent entries read relatively, older ones
      by date.
  - title: Two honest measures, and no third
    body: >-
      A streak counts days you opened the Word. A coverage meter counts
      chapters you actually finished, against the real canon. Neither is
      derived from the other, because a glance at Genesis 1 is not a chapter
      read and should not be counted as one.
  - title: A verse on your home screen
    body: >-
      Pin any verse to a native widget on iOS or Android. The text is resolved
      before it is pinned, so the widget shows scripture rather than a
      placeholder waiting on a network call.
  - title: Four translations, all public domain
    body: >-
      The Berean Standard Bible, King James Version, World English Bible and
      American Standard Version, each credited in the app. Scripture is never
      behind the subscription — we gate our own work, never the text.
  - title: Yours before you are anyone
    body: >-
      No sign-up wall. The app opens straight into scripture and makes an
      anonymous account on first launch. Adding an email later upgrades the
      account you already have rather than starting a second one.

The app started from one number: a phone gets about two hours of attention a
day. ScriptureFlow does not try to argue anyone out of that habit — it borrows
the shape of it. The vertical feed, the swipe, the tap-to-expand: all of it is
the interaction pattern people already have, pointed at a text that rewards
slowing down.

What it will not do is gamify devotion. Streaks count days you showed up, and a
missed day resizes the plan instead of resetting it to zero. There are no
badges, no leaderboards, and no notification telling you that you have let
anybody down.

Everything described above is built and working. What is left is not
engineering: a store review queue, and an audio licence for narration that is
finished but switched off until the recordings are cleared for the translation
on screen.
